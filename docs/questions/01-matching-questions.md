# Matching Questions

**Format:** "Is your nearest ____ the same as my ____?"
**Answer:** yes / no
**Time to answer:** 5 minutes
**Hider reward:** draw 3 cards, keep 1

## General rules

- Useful at any stage of the game, but seekers usually need to reposition first to make them effective.
- Anything outside the map boundary is treated as nonexistent. If no valid location of that type exists inside the boundary, the result is a **null answer** — it still counts as an answered question, and the hider still draws cards.
- Where a category is ambiguous, it falls to the seekers to define what they mean when they ask.
- Unless stated otherwise, distance to a place is measured from its **map icon**.

## Transit

### Commercial Airport
Tie-break for ambiguous cases: it counts as commercial if flights to or from it show up on Google Flights.

### Transit Line
Seekers may only ask this while actually aboard the transit vehicle, and it must be in motion.
- Answer is **yes** if the seekers' current vehicle would stop at the hider's station.
- Best practice: seekers screenshot the full list of stops and send it to the hider.
- An express or limited-stop service that runs through the hider's station without stopping is a **no**. Yes requires an actual scheduled stop.

### Station Name's Length
Count the characters in the station name as it appears in your mapping app. Spaces and hyphens are included. If the app's name contains the word "station," that is part of the count.

### Street or Path
- A street/path ends at the point where the name changes — including a change like "Jet Lag St. East" to "Jet Lag St. West."
- Unnamed streets/paths start and end at their intersections.

## Administrative Divisions

### 1st Administrative Division
The top-level formal division. US → states; Switzerland → cantons; Japan → prefectures.

### 2nd Administrative Division
One level finer. US → counties; Switzerland → districts; Japan → subprefectures.

### 3rd Administrative Division
Another level down; in the US, Switzerland and Japan this is the municipality. Municipal borders can be fuzzy, so seekers must resolve any ambiguity.

### 4th Administrative Division
Doesn't exist everywhere, but many large cities have one — e.g. boroughs in New York City, districts in Zurich, special wards in Tokyo.

## Natural

### Mountain
Whatever your mapping app correctly classifies as a mountain. Measure from the map icon.

### Landmass
A continuous piece of land not separated by a waterway. If the hider's landmass is entirely enclosed within the seekers' landmass, that counts as a match.

The rulebook asks players to be reasonable here — edge cases (is Cape Cod its own landmass or a peninsula cut by an artificial canal?) are genuinely arguable. Agree on any odd local geography before the game starts; during play, seekers resolve ambiguity when asking.

### Park
Whatever your mapping app correctly classifies as a park. Measure from the map icon.

Note the known quirk: measuring to the icon means you can be standing inside a huge park yet be nearer the icon of a smaller park nearby, forcing you to name the small one. The rulebook accepts this outcome because icon-measuring is the most objective method available.

## Places of Interest

All of these use your mapping app's own categorization and are measured from the map icon.

- **Amusement Park**
- **Zoo**
- **Aquarium**
- **Golf Course** — outdoor courses only. Mini golf and driving ranges are excluded. Seekers are urged to spell out what they count when sending this question.
- **Museum**
- **Movie Theater**

---

## Public Utilities

Same rule: mapping app categorization, measured from the map icon.

- **Hospital**
- **Library**
- **Foreign Consulate** — honorary consulates don't count.
