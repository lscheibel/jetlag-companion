A marker should say who, not file a report

Today every player on the map carries three stacked white boxes — a dot, a name chip, and a line of telemetry that reads 3 min ago · ±50 m. It is the debug harness wearing a map. Five proposals, each drawing a different line between what a marker is and what you tap to find out: a pin, a team badge, a map that only speaks when something is wrong, dots with the words in a rail, and edges that point at whoever left the screen. Every one keeps m2-spec §7's visibility table untouched — this deck changes rendering, never who may see whom.

1

Three stacked boxes for one person

A dot, a name chip and an age chip, in a flex-col with min-h-11. One player is 44 px of white paper on a map somebody is trying to read the streets of; five players is a pile of receipts. The marker occupies more of the screen than the block it is standing on.

2

±50 m under every marker, forever

Accuracy is printed at every age below cold, whatever it says. A fix good to twelve metres does not need six characters to say so — it is the default. The number only changes a decision when it is bad, and that is the one case the label does not distinguish.

3

Two truths joined by an interpunct, plus · offline

3 min ago · ±50 m is a record printed with a separator, not a sentence — and connection state is appended to it as a third field with {!player.online && " · offline"}. Being offline is not a footnote on a timestamp; it is a different thing from being stale, and here they are one string.

4

The chips are hardcoded white paper

bg-white/90, border-white, bg-white/80 — the one part of the app that never got role tokens. In the dark theme the map goes to #000 and the markers stay as five white labels, with text-ink-dim (a pale grey) printed on them. That line is close to unreadable at night, which is when half a round is played.

5

The team arrives as two half-signals

A colour in the dot, an emoji trailing the name inside the chip, and the team's name nowhere at all. The house rule is that colour, emoji and name travel together as one object — TeamBadge exists and the map is the screen that promise was written for. On the marker it is currently taken apart.

6

And the sheet behind it is literally a definition list

Tap a marker: Role / Connection / Last seen / Position / Accuracy / Battery — six column names in a <dl>. It reads as an inspector because it is one. "Connection: online" is a schema field with a label on it, not something a person standing on a platform wants to be told.

·

Underneath all six: the map answers a question nobody asked

It reports accuracy, connection and staleness in full, and it never once says how far away anybody is — the single thing a player looks at this screen to find out. The detail is maximal and the answer is missing.

Five ways out

What is a marker allowed to say?

The five differ in where the line falls between the map and a tap. A puts identity on the map and everything else behind one. B makes the team the marker, not the person. C keeps names but earns every other glyph, so a healthy map is silent. D strips the map to dots and moves every word into one rail. E leaves the middle of the map alone and fixes its edges.

All five keep the settled hider bar at the foot of the screen — nearest seeker team, its distance and its own freshness — and all five drop battery and accuracy off the marker entirely. Details in the All five band.

A

The pin

Proposed

One object per player and nothing beside it: a team-coloured disc with the team's emoji in it and the player's initial on the corner. No text on the map at all. Age becomes the pin's own rim — solid, then dashed, then hollow — and every number moves into the sheet.

Age
The rim, not a label. Solid → thin → dashed and desaturated at 2 min → hollow past 10 min, which reads as "was here" rather than "is here". Offline adds a struck line in the alert colour.
Detail
Tap. Name, team, distance and bearing, when it was seen — the rewritten sheet from the last band.
Crowding
Unsolved, as m2-spec §12 leaves it — but five pins pile better than five three-box stacks, because a pin is round and 30 px.

Why: a marker's whole job is who and where, and both of those fit inside one shape. Everything the current label prints is a follow-up question, and follow-up questions belong behind a tap — which is already built.

Cost: two people on the same team are two identical pins distinguished by one letter, and identical initials collide. It also hides age behind a tap, and age is the fact a search is actually run on — C and D both refuse to do that.

B

The team is the marker

Proposed

People standing together are one fact. Members within about 200 m collapse into a single team badge — emoji, count, and one rim dot per member carrying that member's own freshness — over a faint disc showing how spread out they are. They split back into pins when they separate.

Crowding
Five markers at Ostkreuz becomes three. The only proposal that answers §12 rather than living with it.

Why: this game is played by teams. Three seekers walking out of the same station are one thing happening, and drawing them as three things makes the reader do the grouping. It also makes the busiest moment — the start of a round — the moment the map gets simpler instead of worse.

Cost: a centroid is a lie unless the spread is drawn with it, and it is new state to get right: thresholds, hysteresis so a badge does not flicker apart and back at 199 m, and a teammate who stayed behind while the others moved on. More machinery than any other proposal.

C

Quiet until something is wrong

Proposed

Keeps names, but makes every other glyph conditional. A fact that is always true is not information: a fresh fix says nothing, a two-minute-old one says 2 min, an offline one breaks its rim. The map is silent while everything works and speaks exactly where it does not.

Names
Your own team only — the people you coordinate with by name. Everybody else is colour plus emoji, which is who their team is, which is all a rival is to you.
Age
Nothing under 2 min. From 2 min the label is the age — 14 min — in the stale colour. Past 10 min the pin goes hollow and the label says when it was last seen.
Accuracy
Never a number on the map. Above about 100 m the pin sits on a soft disc instead, because a bad fix is a shape, not a measurement.
Offline
Its own treatment — struck rim and the word, in the alert colour — rather than a third field appended to the age.

Why: the current label prints five facts to tell you nothing is wrong. Deviation is what a glance is for, and it is exactly what m2-spec §5 wants staleness to buy: a player should find the quiet marker without reading anything.

Cost: silence is ambiguous until you know the convention — a player new to it cannot tell "fresh" from "the app broke". Wants the legend the bottom bar can carry, and the ping animation on a fresh own-team pin is doing quiet work that reduced-motion turns off.

Marker
19 px team disc with the emoji. Nothing else, ever. The selected one gets a signal-yellow ring rather than a label.
The rail
One card per visible player, nearest first: team chip, name, distance in mono, a freshness bar and its age in words. Horizontally scrollable, three and a bit on screen.
Absence
A player with no position has no marker anywhere, and this is where they can finally be said out loud — a greyed card reading not opened the game, which no proposal above can express.
Relation
The settled hider bar generalised: instead of collapsing to the nearest team, it is the whole list, one card per person, already sorted by the thing players ask about.

Why: comparing is a list job — who is closest, who has gone quiet — and geography is a map job. Split that way each does its own well, and the map itself finally goes quiet without hiding anything behind a tap.

Cost: the bottom of the screen is the most contested space in the app — the rail of tools, the Ask pill, the ruled-out figure — and this puts a scroller across all of it. It also draws everyone twice, so the two surfaces have to stay in step, and a 19 px disc with an emoji in it is small in sun.

Nobody leaves the screen

ProposedAdditive — pairs with A or C

All five

Seven changes that are not about the layout

These hold whichever shape wins, and three of them are small enough to land before one is picked.

Proposed

Accuracy stops being printed per marker

One ring, around your own phone, as m2-spec §5 already argues. For everybody else it is a number in the sheet — and on the map only when it is bad enough to change what you would do, and then as a shape rather than six characters.

Settled in §7

Battery is not map furniture

The spec already says it is dropped once a fix greys out. It follows that it never belonged on the marker in the first place: it is a fact about a phone, read when you have stopped to worry about somebody, which is the sheet.

Proposed

The sheet becomes a person, not a <dl>

One sentence — Mira, Fuchsbau. 340 m north-east, seen 2 minutes ago — then the two things you might do: measure to her, or fly there. Role, connection, coordinates and accuracy fold under a details line for the one person in ten who wants them.

Proposed

Offline and no-position stop sharing a string

Two different absences. Offline is a marker still standing where it last was, saying so. No position ever has no marker at all and belongs in a list — which today means it is invisible, and in D is finally sayable.

Proposed

Staleness gets a shape, not only an opacity

Solid, then dashed, then hollow. The current fade is opacity plus grayscale, which is two forms of one channel — it fails in sun, it fails on a dark map, and it fails for anybody who does not see the desaturation.

Proposed

The marker gets role tokens like everything else

bg-white/90 and border-white are the last hardcoded colours on this screen. --surface and --hairline already do this job in both themes, and the night map is the case the current values were never checked against.

Proposed

Distance from you appears somewhere, for everyone

It is the question the screen exists to answer and no surface currently answers it. Where it lands is the main thing that separates these five — the sheet (A, B, C), the rail (D) or the edge (E).

Needs your call

Four things I could not settle from the specs

Question

Do names belong on the map at all?

A and D say no, C says own team only, today says always. It hangs on whether players think in names or in teams once a round is running. My read is teams for rivals and names for the people you are texting anyway — which is C — but you have played this and I have not.

Question

Is crowding solved now, or still deferred?

§12 accepted the pile on purpose, to be designed against a real concourse. B is the answer to it and also the most machinery in the deck. If the answer is "not yet", B becomes a later layer on top of A or C rather than a fifth option.

Question

Does the hider's map get a different treatment from the seeker's?

A seeker sees two or three teammates; a hider sees everybody in the game. The same marker has to work at both densities, and D's rail earns its bottom space on a hider's phone in a way it may not on a seeker's, where three cards list the people standing next to them.

Question

Should E just be added to whichever wins?

It is the only proposal that adds rather than removes, and it answers the one thing all four others leave to a pinch-out. If it is not a fifth option but a second layer, the real choice here is between four.
