Why a select. Switching family is rare; picking a blank is constant, so the board keeps the screen and the switch keeps a word. The sheet is also the only place a family that is not in this game can explain itself in a sentence instead of a struck-through icon.

What it costs. Two taps to change family instead of one, and the current family is a word you read rather than a lit segment you glance at. Both were judged worth the screen.

Six boards

Every family, drawn from the rulebook you wrote down

Lists, wording and distances come from docs/questions. Every board keeps the full screen, because the family sits in the app bar rather than in a rail across it.

The board is medium — Berlin, team Eule hunting Fuchsbau. Questions this size does not normally have are not greyed out where they lie: they are lifted into a section of their own at the foot of the board, under a dashed rule. Lifted is not blocked — they open the same card as anything else, with one line saying which board size they belong to.

The last screen

The card stays, and the new file is why

Picking a blank fills the sentence; the board’s button opens the card, and the card sends it. That extra beat is where the rules live — and 01-matching-questions.md turned out to be mostly rules: a transit-line question may only be asked from a vehicle that is moving, an express running through the station without stopping is a no, the word “station” counts toward the name length, a landmass enclosed by yours still matches.

None of that fits under a chip. One line on the card, only where the rulebook has a trap in it, and only for the question actually being asked.

01 · Answered

01-matching-questions.md checks out

Board B2 needs no change: same twenty subjects, same five groups. The file’s new material is rule text — a transit-line question may only be asked from a moving vehicle, an express passing through is a no, the word “station” counts toward the name length, an enclosed landmass is a match. All of it lands on the card.

02 · Divisions

Local words, or the rulebook’s numbering

Drawn as State · Berlin, District · Mitte, Neighbourhood · Moabit — readable, and a per-country table somebody has to write and keep right. The rulebook’s “2nd administrative division” needs no table and nobody says it out loud.

The file makes this sharper: a fourth level “doesn’t exist everywhere”, and Berlin is its own awkward case — a city that is also a state. Whatever the wording, a board should list the levels this map actually has and no more.

03 · Settled

Lifted items answer a tap, and can be asked

They stay drawn inert — dashed, no chevron, reason on the line — and they open the ordinary question card with one extra line: this is a large-board question and this board is medium. Screen X3. Nothing is blocked. The separation sorts the menu and states the rule; whether to ask anyway is the players’ call, not the app’s.

04 · Settled

No board judges a choice

The “past the edge” mark under the big radar rungs is gone. A board lists what can be asked and what it costs; whether a question is worth asking is the seeker’s job, and the moment one screen starts rating options every screen has to.
