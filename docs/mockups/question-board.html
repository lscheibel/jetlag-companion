Three pickers

Six families, and the control that moves between them

The icon rail from deck 09 works and reads as a compromise: six abbreviations across 306 px, none of them a word anyone says out loud. Each of these takes a different position on what the switch is for — a menu you open, a bar under your thumb, or no control at all.

All three are drawn on the radar board, with the distances as they now stand in docs/questions/03-radar-questions.md.

Why this is my pick. Switching family is rare; picking a blank is constant. This is the only one of the three that spends its screen accordingly, and the sheet is where a size-excluded family can explain itself in a full sentence rather than a struck-through icon.

What it costs. Two taps to change family instead of one, and the current family is a word in a bar rather than a lit segment — you have to read it to know where you are.

Why it might still win. One tap, always, and the seeker never has to remember what family they are in — it is lit under their thumb. On a screen operated one-handed on a moving train, that is not nothing.

What it costs. The abbreviations. “Measure”, “Therm”, “Tentacle” at 7.5 px is the smallest type in the app, and it sits next to the largest button. It also puts a six-way switch directly beside Ask, which is the one control that spends a question.

Why it is worth drawing. It gives the board every pixel and makes the sentence the whole interface. Between two families it is the fastest thing here — no target to hit, just a thumb moving sideways.

What it costs, and the risk. Going from radar to tentacle is five swipes or a tap on a 3 px tick. The board is also full of horizontally-arranged chips, so a sideways drag that starts on one is ambiguous — and this is a phone being used while walking. I would not ship it as the only way to change family.

Two more screens

A family can be missing too, and search is its own place

And the search you asked about: reached from one line in the picker, it is deck 09’s layout B, unchanged.

Board B2 is drawn from the fan rulebook page instead. Twenty subjects in five groups, including four administrative levels, landmass and station-name length — worth a check against what you meant to write down.

02 · Divisions

Local words, or the rulebook’s numbering

Matching and measuring both lean on administrative levels. Drawn as State · Berlin, District · Mitte, Neighbourhood · Moabit — readable, and a per-country table somebody has to write and keep right. The rulebook’s “2nd administrative division” needs no table and no seeker says it out loud.

03 · Lifted items

Do they respond to a tap?

Drawn inert: dashed, no chevron, reason on the line. The alternative is a tap that opens the full rule and says which board size has it — more to build, and it makes a thing you cannot ask feel like a thing you can.

04 · Past the edge

Should radar comment on a pointless rung?

80 and 160 km cover this whole board, so a yes tells you nothing. Drawn with a two-word mark under the chip. It is the only place any board judges a choice, and that is either a kindness or the app playing for you.
