Two lobbies

The host arranges the room. Everybody else joins it.

Same game, same moment, different job. The host is composing teams and watching for what is in the way; a player wants to know which team they are on, change it if it is wrong, and say they are ready. Giving both the same screen would mean eight people scrolling past controls they will never touch.

Two different jobs

A team is a name and a face. Who is on it is another question.

These were one drawer and it was doing too much: renaming a team and moving a person between teams have nothing in common except appearing near each other. Splitting them gives the identity sheet room for the pickers and gives membership a screen that can show everybody at once — including the people who are not on a team yet.

The one refusal

Blocking, and the much longer list of things that only get a remark

The app has no opinion it is willing to enforce once a round is running — hand limits, curse costs and hiding zones all warn and carry on. The lobby is different, because the four conditions below are not rules anybody agreed to. They are the difference between a game and a broken one.

Everything else is a remark. Lopsided teams, somebody temporarily offline, one seeker team against three hiders — all of it gets said out loud and none of it stands in the way. Deciding a five-against-one is a bad idea is the group’s job, not the app’s.
Before the whistle

Ready is something a person says

Not a team, and not the host on their behalf. Everybody marks themselves — after one screen showing what they are marking themselves ready for — grouped under the team they are on so the gaps are obvious at a glance. The host gets the start only once the last person has said yes.

Ready
Per person, tapped by that person. A team is ready when everyone on it is, and that is arithmetic rather than a second action.
Starting
Held, not tapped. It begins the hiding countdown for nine people at once, which deserves 700 ms of intent.
The code
Behind the share control on every lobby screen, host or not. Anybody can pull a friend in.
House rules
Free text the host wrote. Read-only for everybody else, shown once before being ready, and reachable in two taps for the rest of the game.
