Sixty-two questions, one thumb, a train in four minutes.

The seeker taps Ask, picks the hider team, and then has to find one question out of six families — each with its own answer shape, its own wait, and its own price in cards for the team being hunted. Four ways to lay that menu out, drawn against the same game: a medium board in Berlin, team Eule hunting Fuchsbau, 28 minutes on the round clock.

Four proposals

The same sixty-two questions, sorted four ways

Each phone below is the screen that opens after Ask → Fuchsbau. They differ only in how the menu is organised: by family, by nothing at all, by what the answer does to the map, or by keeping every family one tap away at all times. Everything downstream — the question card, the position you ask from, the wait — is shared, and is drawn once further down.

Needs your call

Seven things I had to decide to draw anything at all

Each of these is drawn one way above. None of them is settled, and a different answer changes the menu rather than the styling.

01 · Distances

A clean metric ladder, or the rulebook’s own numbers

Jet Lag’s radar rungs are ¼, ½, 1, 3, 5, 10, 25, 50 and 100 miles. Drawn above as 500 m, 1, 3, 5, 10, 25, 50, 100 and 160 km, which is what the code already does with its three test radii — readable, and a different game by a few hundred metres. Faithful conversion gives 400 m, 800 m, 1.6, 4.8, 8, 16, 40, 80, 160 km.

02 · Cards

Is the hider’s reward in this game at all?

Every screen above prices a question in cards the hider draws. If Zero Lag is not running the deck, all six price chips come off and layout C loses the counterweight to its percentages — the menu gets simpler and the choice gets flatter.

03 · Percentages

How far the app is allowed to help

Layout C’s share estimates are the difference between a menu and an advisor. Ship them, ship the grouping without them, or keep the whole shape for a later screen that only appears when a seeker asks for a suggestion.

04 · Divisions

“District”, or “2nd administrative division”

Drawn with local words and examples — State · Berlin, District · Mitte, Neighbourhood · Moabit. That is a per-country table somebody has to write, and it is wrong the moment the game is played in Zürich. The alternative is the rulebook’s numbering, which is exactly the technical vocabulary we agreed to keep out.

05 · Thermometer

A question that finishes somewhere else

You announce it, you travel, then you send the second position. Above it is one row in the menu and a countdown that does not fit it. It may deserve to leave the menu entirely and live on the map as a tool you start and stop, like the ruler.

06 · Photos

Ten minutes, twenty, and where the picture lands

Photo questions are the only ones whose answer is not a constraint. They need somewhere to arrive — a card in the same list with the image in it — and the large-board variants are timed differently. None of that is drawn.

07 · Board size

Lock and explain, or hide

Tentacle does not exist on a small board and photo grows with the board. Drawn locked with the reason showing, so the menu teaches the game. Hiding them makes a shorter screen and a seeker who never learns why their friend’s game had a question theirs did not.

Where I would start

B, with D’s tab rail doing the filtering

One list is the only layout that stays fast on the fourth question of the evening, and the family chips at its top are D’s rail with a different job — one build, not two. Keep A’s sentences as the headings so the list still teaches, and hold C back for a “what should I ask?” screen once the deduction engine can price a question honestly.
