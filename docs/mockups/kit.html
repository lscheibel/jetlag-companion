Roles, not colours. Touch floors, not sizes.

Weight
Switch the whole page between bold, fill and duotone with the control in the masthead — it repoints every icon on every screen, so a weight can be judged on the lobby and the map rather than on a grid of specimens.
Reading
bold holds up at 17px and beside Bricolage 800. fill gains weight but loses the inner detail that tells a crosshair from a radius. duotone puts a 20% wash behind the stroke, which reads on a surface and disappears on the action yellow.
Size
17px inside rows, chips and the tool strip · 20px on step buttons · 24px in a door's glyph tile. Never scaled between those.
Colour
currentColor, always. An icon takes the colour of the control it sits in, which is what lets one glyph work on the action yellow and on a dark surface.

No icon travels alone. Every one of these appears beside its label — in a tool strip, a tab, a row — except inside a 44px map control, where the label lives in aria-label and the control is one of five a player learns once. A glyph is a shortcut for something already named, never the name itself.

02 · Motion

Nine loops, one vocabulary.

Continuous, decorative motion is CSS; motion that reacts to state or has an exit to animate is Motion. Both take their easings from the same tokens, which is what keeps a screenful of small animations reading as one system rather than fifteen separately tuned curves. Every one of them stops dead under prefers-reduced-motion.

Two changes this deck proposes to the shipped system. --ease-pop peaks at 1.03 here instead of 1.08, and the scale in zl-pop drops from 1.14 to 1.06 — at the old values a chosen tile lurches rather than acknowledges. And anything that travels — a segmented thumb, a tab highlight, a sheet — moves on --ease-travel instead, a curve with no overshoot at all: overshoot belongs to things that land, not to things that slide.

The rule for what is Motion and what is CSS: a dismissed thing should be seen leaving. That single requirement is why Sheet and the floating Notice are Motion components and everything else here is CSS.

03 · Actions

Things that happen when a thumb lands.

Every action is lifted on its own darker edge rather than on translucent black — over a dark ground a black shadow is no shadow at all, and a flat red button beside a raised yellow one reads as disabled rather than as dangerous. The press is CSS, not a spring: a button has to answer on the frame it is touched.

05 · Pickers

A team is a name, a colour and a face — always all three.

Colour never carries meaning alone. The palette is eight Okabe–Ito hues, so they stay distinguishable under all three common colour vision deficiencies, and the emoji are silhouettes that survive being 16px on a map marker.

ColorPicker

Eight swatches, not a colour wheel: this is read on a phone in direct sun. Taken colours are struck through rather than hidden, so the grid never reflows under a thumb.

08 · Frame

Every screen is a step in something longer.

A header that can always go back, a body that scrolls, an action area pinned where a thumb is. A fixed-height column, not a growing page — two things stuck to the bottom of a growing page are two things stuck to the same place, which is how a tab bar ends up on top of the button it should sit under.

10 · What is missing

Six shapes screens draw by hand today.

Each one already exists somewhere in apps/web, written out more than once. That is the signal that it belongs in the kit — not that a component library should have one.

ComponentDrawn by hand inWhy it should move
Checkboxroutes/g.$code.setup.transit.tsxPicking several things out of a list is currently a switch, which promises the change happens now. It doesn't — the modes apply at review.
SegmentedControlcomponents/theme.tsx · lobby · map-tool-sheet.tsxThree hand-rolled versions of one control. The travelling thumb is what makes it read as one thing, and only one of the three has it.
ToggleButtonsetup/area/tool-strip.tsx · map/map-rail.tsxTwo strips, two sets of pressed styles. One component with aria-pressed and a tone covers the tool strip, the filter row and the HUD.
ColorPicker · EmojiPickerlobby/team-drawer.tsxThe selected-swatch ring is fiddly — double ring, ground colour, pop — and it is about to be needed again for pin colours on the map.
Iconglyphs typed inline: ▦ ✎ ◎ ⇪ ━ ◉ ☰ ⋯Every strip in the app draws its icons as literal characters, which render differently on each phone and cannot take a weight. @phosphor-icons/react at weight="bold" replaces all of them; this page is drawn with it.
IconButtonmap/map-interactions.tsxMapHudButton is already the shared one; it just lives in the map folder, so the lobby header re-implements it at a different size.

Everything else on this page ships from packages/ui today. The kit has no Modal, no Dropdown, no Tooltip and no Toast — a sheet covers what a modal would, a segmented control covers what a dropdown would, and anything worth a tooltip is worth a line of text under the control instead.
