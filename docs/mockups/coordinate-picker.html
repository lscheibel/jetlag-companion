A point is a fact until you touch it.

Settled: layout D, everywhere. In a card a point is a 44 px readout — no border to type into, no caret, no keyboard. Tapping it opens one sheet, and the field only exists in there. This revision puts every source in one list, paste included, moves copy onto the field's trailing edge, and gives places their kind step. Plus codes are out for now.

At rest

In the card there is nothing to type into

One line: where the value came from, the value itself, copy, and a chevron that says there is more behind it. It reads like the pin's label and the pin's note — a fact about the thing being made, not a form field competing with them.

Why no field here. The point is usually already right — it arrived from the map, from your own position, from a place you picked. Typing is the exception, and a card that shows a text box advertises the exception as if it were the job. It also keeps the keyboard down, which on a phone is the difference between seeing the map and not.

The cut editor's two ends. Each line carries its own label, and the one you are working on is the one whose sheet is open — there is no focus ring to maintain, because only one sheet exists at a time.

On tap

One sheet, three levels deep at most

The field lives here, at a size worth typing into, with copy on its trailing edge. Under it, the sources — every one of them a row, paste included. Two rows lead somewhere: pins are one list, and places are a kind first and then a place, the same two steps the radius tool already asks for. Switch levels below.

The point sheet

Live, no save

Nothing in here is staged. Every source writes the point and dismisses the sheet, so closing is something that happens to you rather than something you do — and there is no button that means "yes, really".

Why the lists live in this sheet. Picking a pin is picking a coordinate — the same errand as typing one — and sending it to a sheet of its own would make the back gesture mean two different things. One sheet opens, and it always closes back to the map.

Hiding zone stop is the hider's committed stop, not a zone drawn on the map and not a stop they are still considering. Before they commit there is nothing to offer, so the row is not there.

Moved

It belongs with the sources, not beside the field: like the current location, it produces a point outright and the sheet leaves. The browser will not hand over the clipboard without a gesture, and a row is a gesture.

The rule that falls out of this. The sheet closes when a source succeeds. A source that fails has something to say, so it stays and says it.

End to end

Every source, counted from a card at rest

Most sources are two taps. The ones that cost more are the ones that are a choice from a list, and the deepest — a place — is a kind and then a place, which is the price of not scrolling 2,600 bus stops. The thing you do most often to a point you did not set, copying it, never opens anything.

From round one

What this replaces

Three layouts that kept the field in the card. Each was cheaper in taps and more expensive in everything else; the numbers are the card height one point costs.

A · 71 px

Value bar

One 52 px row with the sources behind its glyph. Fastest to type into, but every card carried a live text field and a caret waiting for a keyboard.

B · 128 px

Value bar and rail

The same row over a strip of source tiles. Everything one tap away and nothing hidden — at nearly three times the card height, twice over in the cut editor.

C · 94 px for two

Collapsed line

44 px per point, expanding in place. Closest to what survives: its closed line is this readout. What it did on opening now happens in the sheet, where the map is not underneath it.

Settled

What this deck now fixes

The decisions the drawings above depend on, so a later reading of them is not a re-litigation.

Live, with no save

Typing writes straight through to the card. There is no draft, no "use this point", nothing to confirm and nothing to cancel.

Settled · 31 Aug 2026

A source that lands closes the sheet

Current location, paste, a pin, a place, the zone stop. A paste that cannot be read is the exception: the text stays, the field goes red, the sheet stays open.

Rows that cannot work are not there

No fix yet, no "Current location". No pins, no "From a pin". No committed zone, no "Hiding zone stop" — and seekers never see that row at all.

Settled · 31 Aug 2026

Hiding zone stop means the committed stop

The hider's own committed hiding stop, not a zone drawn on the map and not a stop they are still turning over.

Settled · 31 Aug 2026

Kinds show what the board carries

Every kind on the board appears, sorted by distance whatever that distance is — the same rule the map's own layer picker follows.

No plus codes

Out of scope for now. The field is lat/lng, and the parser keeps the syntaxes it already reads.
