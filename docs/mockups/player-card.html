UZero Lag·Deck 13·The player card·20 Sep 2026

Six labelled rows, and not one of them is the answer

Tapping a marker is a question with a short answer — how far, how long ago — and today it opens a definition list of six rows that are all the same size, in the screen's own vocabulary, with nothing to do at the end of it. Tapping your own marker opens something else entirely: a different component, with a title and two lines of coordinates. Three proposals, each with a different idea of what the card is about. A says it is a position. B says it is a person, over time. C says it is a team. All three fold the two cards into one, because you are a player who happens to be yourself.

Today

A table where a headline should be

Drawn from apps/web/src/map/player-sheet.tsx and own-position.tsx as they stand. Nothing on either is wrong — every row is a fact m2-spec asked for. What is wrong is that they are the same size as each other, that they are labelled in the language of the code, and that there are two of them.

1

It is the one map card that is not a sheet

A hand-rolled <section> pinned to the bottom edge, with a Close button where every sibling has a grabber, a scrim and a drag. It is also rendered as {selected && …}, so it unmounts on close and never animates out — the exact thing useHeldValue exists in the kit to prevent.

2

The team is a badge with nothing around it

PlayerTeamBadge sits between the name and the table carrying a name, an emoji and a colour, and says nothing about what that team is — which side, how many, where the rest of them are. On a map about chasing a team, that is the fact with the most left to give.

3

Six rows, one size, and the answer is third

How far away they are and how long ago is the whole reason a thumb went to that marker. It is row three of six, in the same 14 px as Role, under a heading that reads like a column name.

4

A position, and no distance from you

The stop sheet has DistanceToYou. The amenity sheet has DistanceToYou. The card about a person you are chasing gets coordinates and a Copy button — and fromYou is already in scope, passed to the two sheets rendered directly above it.

5

It speaks the screen's vocabulary, not a player's

Connection, Accuracy, Role: not assigned — and fix.source printed raw beside the metres, so a card in a player's hand says fused. The house rule is no technical vocabulary in user-facing text.

6

Nothing to do at the end of it

Every other sheet on this map ends in an action. This one ends. You cannot measure to them, centre on them, isolate their trail or open their team from here.

7

And yourself is a different object entirely

Your own marker opens OwnPositionSheet: a title and two lines of coordinates. No team, no age, no battery, no word about whether your background tracker is the thing reporting you. The one player who can see everything about themselves gets the poorest card in the app.

·

Underneath all seven: it repeats the marker

The marker already carries the name, the emoji, the staleness and positionLabel — age and accuracy, in one line, on the map. The card restates all of it at greater length and adds two facts: a clock time and a battery percentage. A card that costs a tap has to be worth more than the label it covers.

Three ways out

A position, a person, or a team

Each proposal answers one question differently: what is this card about? A says it is about a position, and gives the one number the size it deserves. B says it is about a person over time, and puts the trail that is already on the map into words. C says it is about a team, because that is what you chase — and folds A inside it.

All three: one component for everybody including you, the kit's Sheet, distance from you, the labels gone, and a card that ends in something to do. Details in the last band but one.

A

The readout

Proposed

Keeps the sheet and throws away the table. One fact gets the headline and everything else becomes a chip. The trick is which fact: while the position is fresh, the headline is the distance; once it is cold, the headline is the age — because a distance to a forty-minute-old position is a distance to where somebody used to be.

Proposed

Why. The trail is already drawn and nearly unreadable — three of them crossing at Ostkreuz is a scribble. As a list it becomes a deduction: Treptower Park at 17:04, Warschauer at 17:19, so they are on the Ringbahn going north. What it costs. Naming each vertex needs a nearest-stop lookup per row — real work, and wrong-looking the moment somebody is in a park. It is a long card for a teammate standing next to you. And it makes a hider's movement much easier to read, which is a game-balance change dressed as a layout.

C

The team

Proposed

You do not chase a person; you chase a team. Tapping any marker opens that team — members nearest first, each with its own distance and its own freshness — with the one you tapped opened in place. It is the shape the hider map's bottom card already settled on, and it contains A rather than replacing it.

Why. It matches what the game is about — a hider trio moves as one, and a seeker converging on one of them is converging on all three. It is the same shape the hider map's bottom card already settled on. And it is the first thing on this map that begins to answer the crowding that m2-spec §12 knowingly deferred: five markers in one concourse are usually one team.

What it costs. Two taps to a fact A gives in zero, and the card you get is not the card you asked for — you tapped a person. A team of one is a header and a row. And a team whose positions are withheld is a card of names with no numbers at all, which is either honest or useless depending on who you ask.

All three

Seven changes that are not about the layout

These hold whichever shape wins. Three of them are small enough to land on their own before a shape is picked.

Proposed

One card, and you are in it

PlayerSheet and OwnPositionSheet become one component. You are a player with isSelf, and the differences — your accuracy ring, your compass, your tracker, no distance-from-yourself — are branches inside one card rather than a second, poorer one. Two components rendering one idea drift; the kit already makes this argument about TeamBadge.

Settled elsewhere

It becomes a Sheet

Grabber, scrim, drag-to-dismiss, and useHeldValue so it animates out with the content it had. The Close button goes: the gesture is the dismiss, and every other card on this map already knows that.

Proposed

Distance from you, like every other map sheet

DistanceToYou is on the stop sheet and the amenity sheet. It is missing from the one card about a person, and fromYou is already in scope at the call site. This is a one-line fix worth making today whichever shape wins.

Proposed

The labels go

Connection becomes a dot and a word. Accuracy becomes ±22 m beside the age, where the marker already puts it. Role becomes the side, next to the team, where it means something. And fix.source stops being printed raw — fused is a word this codebase uses, not a word a player does.

Needs your call

"No position" stops meaning two things

Fan-out sends a withheld position as fix: null, fixAgeMs: null — byte-identical to a phone that has never reported. The card currently prints no position for both. "Their phone is off" and "you are not allowed to know" are different facts, and one of them is a rule of the game worth stating out loud.

Needs your call

The card opens from a name, not only from a pin

m2-spec §12 says a player with no position "appears in the roster panel instead". There is no roster panel, so every player you may not locate — and every player whose phone is off — is currently unreachable. Whichever card wins needs a second way in.

Settled elsewhere

Battery keeps its three states

Shown while fresh, dropped the moment the marker greys out or the player goes offline, and never a fourth "we knew once" state. m2-spec §7 settled this and none of the three touches it — a chip that is absent is the same rule as a row that is absent.

Needs your call

Four things I could not settle from the specs

Question

Does the card carry a bearing?

A distance with no direction can send a seeker the wrong way down a platform. The compass is already watched for your own marker, and a bearing between two points is trivial. But m2-spec §8 was emphatic that only your own marker has an orientation — that argument was about broadcasting a phone's facing, which an arrow pointing at somebody is not. I think §8 permits it. It is your call whether it meant to.

Question

Does a card about a person belong to a person at all?

C is the only one that says no, and it is the one that matches the game. But it answers a question you did not ask — you tapped Mara and got Vulpes. A and B keep the tap honest and leave the team a chevron away. This is the choice that decides the other two.

Question

Is a readable trail a balance change?

B turns a scribble on the map into a sentence a seeker can act on. The trail is already drawn and already synced, so nothing new is revealed — but making it legible is not nothing. If hiders should be harder to read than that, B is the wrong deck and the trail layer is the thing to revisit.

Question

What happens to Measure?

All three end in Measure to here, which would seed the existing measure tool with a player's position as one end — a moving end, that goes stale while the ruler is open. Either the measurement freezes at the position it was taken from, or it tracks and the card has quietly invented a live pursuit readout. The second is more useful and much more of a decision.
